# A 12-model council plans a launch video (UB-001, 2026-10-05)

## The question

Useful Bot 1.1.1 needed a launch video that makes people install it, by showing what it does that Grok Bot, Meta Muse, OpenAI Dots and OpenBot don't. The owner had rejected a first storyboard as too generic. Could a council of twelve models, all given the same sourced dossier, converge on a concept that is both persuasive and strictly honest about what ships?

## What ran

**Research (Phase 1 and 2), Claude Sonnet 5.5 sub-agents, checked by Claude Opus 5.5:** nine researchers, one each for Grok Bot, Muse, Dots, OpenBot (openbot.run), other close competitors, regional availability, vendor terms for every subscription route, Deepgram TTS, and Remotion tooling. Plus a sourced product fact sheet for 1.1.1 from the code. Official sources first; OpenAI's help center blocked the fetch tool, so the Dots facts were re-read from the official pages with a headless browser. Output: `docs/plan/UB-001-research.md`. Raw dossiers: `research/`.

**Reference screenshots:** a clean Useful Bot Dev profile (the dev state swapped aside to a backup and restored after), light and dark, in `dossier/screenshots/`.

## Numbers

- 12 of 12 members answered round 1. 11 of 12 critiqued in round 2: GLM 5.3 Flash spent its whole 32k reasoning budget and returned nothing; its retry at a low reasoning variant hung with no output and no log line and was stopped after 24 minutes.
- Final picks: 10 of 11 chose a result-first opening after reading two answers that opened on a finished piece of work. 6 of 11 picked the two-bot client update as the demo, and 2 more its sibling, a two-bot sales report. 10 of 11 chose Wade at expressivity 0.
- Claim errors caught in round 2: about a dozen distinct ones (listed in `SYNTHESIS.md`), including two in the chair's answer. The biggest: 5 of 12 round-1 scripts staged an approval card that Auto would never raise (writes inside an attached folder run without a card; verified at `agent/instructions.md:37` and `agent/tools/bash.ts:127-147`).
- Fable found 16 issues in the drafted plan (5 high, 8 medium, 3 low), including one the council missed: with no folder attached, the bot's root is the owner's real home, which would put real Documents in frame and in the model's context.
- Voice samples: all 8 matched the script word for word (STT merges "Useful Bot" into one word in some takes). Measured speaking rates: Cliff 213 wpm and Wade 176 wpm at speed 1.0.

## Conclusion

The council converged hard, and mostly through critique, not round 1. Round 1 produced eleven different demos and five scripts that would have staged UI behaviour the product doesn't have. Round 2's anonymous claim check was the most valuable step: models that had just read a sourced fact sheet were good at catching each other's overclaims, and the best idea (open on a finished result) spread from two answers to ten.

The chair's own round-1 answer had two of the errors the others caught (an over-general "every AI agent wants your cloud" opener and a staged card). Writing the chair answer before reading the others kept the record honest about that.

The adversarial review by a different strong model was worth its cost: Fable found a privacy hole (the real home folder) and a card that would have shown raw HTML on camera, neither of which twelve models had caught, because both needed reading code paths beyond the dossier.

## What changed because of it

- The video concept: result first, a two-bot client update, one approval card on a delete that policy always cards, "your computer" in the voice-over (owner, for a future Windows release), Mac only on the end card, no competitor names, no music.
- The owner picked Cliff by ear over the council's Wade.
- UB-015 was logged: several subscription routes in the app (GLM Coding Plan, Qwen Coding Plan, Command Code, GitHub Copilot) conflict with vendor terms, and ChatGPT moved to OpenAI's official open-source sign-in (PR #187).
- The plan is awaiting the owner's approval before any build.

## Dossier errors (kept for the record)

- `dossier/screenshots/07-settings-dark.png` is a light capture taken before the dark setting applied.
- The round-1 brief left out Flux's speed parameter, which led several members to say it has none.
- Raw Codex and Grok logs are not committed: Codex sessions read local agent config and memory files while exploring, and Grok's raw output holds its thinking. The answers themselves are in `round1/` and `round2/`.
